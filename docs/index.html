Archiving the Present

a community guide to archiving languages, memories & knowledges

An emerging network of language and memory keepers — organised by OpenSpeaks.

Launching 3 September 2026 · Kochi

The Book

“People are Knowledge” — this title of the 2011 documentary by Achal Prabhala, Priya Sen and Zen Marie stayed with us.

From the Preface. Archiving the Present grew out of a three-month online workshop series (18 May–24 August 2026), closing with an in-person session in Kochi. Nearly 20 trainers from seven countries built its eight modules from their own sessions.

Modules

  1. 01

    Planning before documenting

    Ganesh Birua, Ramjit Tudu and Shalini A

    “This module helps you plan your language documentation around your own community’s motivations.”

  2. 02

    Consent, ethics and payment

    Taukeer Alam, Anju Gagrai, Opino Gomango and Puthiya Purayil Sneha

    “This module explains how informed consent, rights, FAIR and CARE-based ethical practice, and payment work together in community language documentation.”

  3. 03

    Recording

    Pius Akumbu and Amrit Sufi

    “This module aims to help you record clear, honest audio and video with the tools you already have, in ways that respect people’s comfort and safety.”

  4. 04

    File managing + editing

    Deepak Kumar Beshra, Amrit Sufi and Subhashish Panigrahi

    “This module is to help you organise your audio and video simply, keep them safe, and do basic editing so your recordings are ready for sharing, archiving, and future use.”

  5. 05

    Metadata

    Yulha Lhawa, Abu Sayeed and Subhashish Panigrahi

    “This module is to help you understand what media and metadata are, and prepare both so that archives, libraries, and other memory institutions can work with the material you collect.”

  6. 06

    Publication

    Eddie Avila, Mithilesh Baraily and Mariana Fossatti

    “This module explains why publishing matters, who should be involved, and simple ways to share language recordings and texts safely and usefully.”

  7. 07

    Archiving the present: language, storytelling, and languages that live

    Smita Khator, Ritu Sharma and Sanjib Chaudhary

    “This module does not see an archive as a one-time act that preserves the past. Instead, it sees it as an ongoing practice that serves your community in the present.”

  8. 08

    Fundraising

    Mandana Seyfeddinipur

    “This module is to help you understand how to fund language documentation work and how institutional archiving works, as two practical skills every community documenter needs.”

Followed by two appendices — a Text Style Guide for captioning, subtitling, and transcribing, and the Oral Knowledge Framework the manual's practice is built on — and a Glossary. Edited by Arky AR, Puthiya Purayil Sneha and Subhashish Panigrahi.

Network

Archiving the Present is an emerging network of language and memory keepers, convened by OpenSpeaks, working together on community-led documentation and archiving.

Convened by

  • OpenSpeaks

    A community network helping Indigenous and marginalised-language communities across South Asia document and archive their own knowledge.

Network members

  • People’s Archive of Rural India (PARI)

    An archive of everyday lives and voices from rural India, in Indian languages.

  • Endangered Languages Archive (ELAR)

    A free, long-term archive for language documentation collections.

  • Bukkafe

    Print publisher and partner press for Archiving the Present.

Supported by

  • Wikimedia Foundation, Samagata Foundation and Creative Commons

Funding

Fundraising is a skill, like recording or archiving — and community documenters can learn it too. A few funders that support language documentation and archiving work directly:

  • Endangered Languages Documentation Programme (ELDP)

    Grants for language documentation fieldwork worldwide, plus Legacy Material Grants for digitising old recordings.

  • Firebird Foundation for Anthropological Research

    Grants up to $10,000, including a supplemental grant for oral literature and traditional ecological knowledge. Deadlines fall on 15 January and 15 June each year.

  • Endangered Language Fund

    Language Legacies grants, open to community members and researchers from any country.

  • Foundation for Endangered Languages

    Small grants, around $1,000, for documentation and revitalisation projects.
